# Truncated Importance Sampling

**Type**: concept  
**Tags**: #concept

## Overview

Truncated Importance Sampling (TIS) is an algorithmic technique for correcting the distribution mismatch between the separate sampler and learner engines used in modern RL training frameworks for LLMs. It was proposed in "Your Efficient RL Framework Secretly Brings You Off-Policy RL Training" (Yao et al., 2025) as a practical fix for a fundamental systems engineering problem.

In modern RL training, rollouts are generated by an optimized inference engine (vLLM, SGLang) and policy updates are computed by a training framework (FSDP, DeepSpeed). Even when both engines use the same model weights, subtle differences in precision, parallelism strategies, and sampling code cause the token probabilities they predict to diverge — sometimes substantially. This means rollouts are drawn from π_sampler (the inference engine) rather than from π_learner (the training engine), secretly turning on-policy RL into off-policy RL.

TIS applies importance sampling theory to correct for this mismatch: the policy gradient is multiplied by the importance ratio π_learner / π_sampler, truncated (capped) at a maximum value ρ for stability. This is directly analogous to the correction term in standard importance-weighted estimators. The approach can be applied on top of any RL optimizer (GRPO, PPO, DAPO).

Key empirical findings:
- TIS significantly boosts performance when using quantized (int8/fp8) rollouts by correcting the increased sampler-learner gap.
- TIS provides additional benefits even with full-precision (bf16) rollouts, likely by marginally improving entropy.
- The dominant contributor to the engine gap is **different parallelism strategies** (e.g., tensor parallelism in sampler vs. sequence parallelism in learner).
- Longer rollouts increase the maximum token-probability mismatch (more opportunities for a single large divergence).

- Adopted by OLMo 3 as part of its improved GRPO recipe.
