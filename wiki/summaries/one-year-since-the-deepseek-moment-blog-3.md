# The Future of the Global Open-Source AI Ecosystem: From DeepSeek to AI+

## Summary

Third and final post in Hugging Face's series on China's open-source AI ecosystem since DeepSeek R1 (after [[One Year Since the "DeepSeek Moment"]] and [[Architectural Choices in China's Open-Source AI Ecosystem: Building Beyond DeepSeek]]), examining specific organizations' trajectories and positing future directions for global open source. DeepSeek is the most-followed organization on Hugging Face overall, and Qwen is fourth. The post profiles several trajectories: Alibaba/Qwen treated open source as ecosystem/infrastructure strategy rather than a single flagship model, reaching 113,000+ derivative models and 200,000+ Qwen-tagged repositories by mid-2025 (more than Meta's Llama or DeepSeek individually); Tencent moved from "borrowing" DeepSeek via product integration to building its own open releases (Hunyuan) in vision/video/3D from May 2025; ByteDance runs an "AI application factory" model, selectively open-sourcing high-value components (UI-TARS-1.5, Seed-Coder, SuperGPQA) while keeping its Doubao app (100M+ DAU by December 2025) as the primary product surface; Baidu shifted from closed-model prioritization toward open release (Ernie 4.5) alongside renewed investment in PaddlePaddle and its Kunlunxin chip.

The post argues "DeepSeek Moments" became normalized: Moonshot, Z.ai, and MiniMax all adjusted rapidly post-R1, with Kimi K2, GLM-4.5, and MiniMax M2 earning places on AI-World's open-model milestone rankings, and Kimi K2's release itself being called "another DeepSeek moment." Application-first companies (Xiaohongshu, Bilibili, Xiaomi, Meituan) began training their own models once strong reasoning became cheaply available via open source, while research institutions (BAAI, Shanghai AI Lab) redirected effort toward toolchains, evaluation systems, and infrastructure (FlagOpen, OpenDataLab, OpenCompass) rather than chasing single-model performance.

Looking forward, the post frames the defining shift as a full chain forming: models extended, deployments reused, hardware/software coordinated, governance embedded, all built atop years of compute investment since 2017's "East Data, West Compute" strategy. China's total compute capacity is cited at ~1590 EFLOPS as of 2025, with AI-specific compute reportedly growing ~43% year-over-year and average data-center PUE improving to ~1.46. The August 2025 "AI+" action plan is framed as a shift from the original 2017 plan's foundation-building toward large-scale deployment and integration, distinct from an AGI-maximalist strategy, with R1 serving as the catalyst that activated already-built compute/energy/data infrastructure.

## Key Claims

- DeepSeek is the most-followed organization on Hugging Face overall; Qwen is fourth.
- By mid-2025, Qwen had over 113,000 derivative models on Hugging Face and 200,000+ repositories tagging it, more than Meta's Llama (27k) or DeepSeek (6k) individually; Alibaba's total derivatives nearly match Google's and Meta's combined.
- ByteDance's Doubao app surpassed 100 million daily active users in December 2025, despite the company's relatively low-profile open-source posture.
- China's total compute capacity is cited at ~1590 EFLOPS as of 2025, with AI-specific ("intelligent") compute capacity reportedly growing ~43% year-over-year and average data-center PUE improving to ~1.46.
- The August 2025 "AI+" action plan is framed as a shift from the 2017 "New Generation AI Development Plan"'s foundation-building toward large-scale deployment/integration, distinct from an AGI-maximalist strategy.
- Kimi K2's open-sourcing is described as "another DeepSeek moment," reportedly accompanying ~$500M in funding raised by Moonshot by end of 2025 (no IPO announced, unlike Z.ai and MiniMax).
