## Summary

Neural Architecture Search (NAS) automates the engineering of neural network topologies, aiming to replace human-designed networks with optimized, task-specific architectures. Historically, network architecture engineering has relied heavily on manual trial-and-error by human experts. NAS systematicizes this process by formalizing a three-pillar framework consisting of: (1) a **Search Space** defining valid layer combinations and topologies, (2) a **Search Algorithm** (such as reinforcement learning, evolutionary strategies, progressive surrogate models, or gradient descent) that proposes candidate models, and (3) an **Evaluation Strategy** that estimates child model performance to provide optimization feedback. Early NAS methods demonstrated remarkable performance but required massive compute budgets, prompting deep research into efficiency-maximizing strategies.

![fig-1](../assets/2020-08-06-nas/fig-1.webp)

To scale NAS to practical engineering workflows, researchers constrained search spaces and optimized evaluation. Transitioning from raw sequential layer-wise spaces to repeated **Cell-based Representations** (e.g., NASNet) drastically shrieked the search space and improved transferability across datasets. Simultaneously, evaluation strategies evolved from training thousands of candidate networks from scratch to reusing weights via parameter sharing. **ENAS (Efficient NAS)** revolutionized this by constructing an over-parameterized supergraph where weights are shared among sampled child subgraphs, cutting search budgets from thousands of GPU-hours to just a single GPU-day.

![fig-15](../assets/2020-08-06-nas/fig-15.webp)

The current state-of-the-art leverages **One-Shot and Differentiable NAS**. Methods like **DARTS** continuously relax the discrete search space using softmax mixing probabilities over candidate operations, formulating the search as a joint bilevel optimization problem over architecture parameters ($\alpha$) and network weights ($w$). **ProxylessNAS** extends this by binarizing path choices stochastically to drop the massive memory footprint of DARTS, and integrates a differentiable latency prediction model to optimize models directly on target hardware. Finally, systems like **AutoML-Zero** push the boundary of automatic design by using regularized/aging neuroevolution to discover entire machine learning algorithms from raw mathematical primitives, successfully reconstructing components like neural networks, gradient descent, and learning rate schedules from scratch.

![fig-20](../assets/2020-08-06-nas/fig-20.webp)

## Key Claims

- **High Computational Overhead of Naive NAS**: Early sequential layer-wise NAS required thousands of GPU days (e.g., Zoph & Le used 800 GPUs for 28 days) because child models were trained to convergence from scratch.
- **Cell-Based modularity**: Structuring search spaces as repeated stacks of Normal and Reduction Cells (pioneered by NASNet) shrinks the search space, allows scaling model size via repeat parameters, and supports drop-in transfers to other datasets.
- **ScheduledDropPath Regularization**: Stochastically dropping paths with a linearly increasing probability during training prevents cell-based architectures from collapsing or overfitting.
- **Parameter Sharing (ENAS)**: Framing the search space as a single over-parameterized supergraph where child subgraphs share weights enables extremely fast evaluation, achieving 1000x efficiency gains.
- **Differentiable Relaxation**: DARTS maps discrete operational decisions to continuous parameters by softmax-weighting edges, allowing the model to be optimized via gradient descent rather than reinforcement learning.
- **Hardware-Aware Latency Pruning**: ProxylessNAS utilizes stochastically binarized paths to bypass DARTS' high memory usage and incorporates a differentiable hardware latency loss function ($F(\cdot)$) to search directly on target mobile/edge devices without proxy datasets.
- **Algorithm Evolution from Primitives**: AutoML-Zero demonstrates that regularized neuroevolution can build complex ML components (Setup, Learn, Predict) from basic mathematical operations, uncovering classic algorithms (e.g., AdaGrad, linear networks) without human bias.

## Questions & Gaps

- **Weight Entanglement in Supergraphs**: How severe is the performance rank correlation distortion between child subgraphs in shared-parameter environments? While ENAS reports good empirical performance, subsequent studies have questioned the actual rank correlation between weights evaluated inside the supergraph vs. trained from scratch.
- **Bilevel Optimization Instability**: DARTS' bilevel optimization (alternating updates to $w$ and $\alpha$) is notoriously sensitive to hyperparameters and can suffer from optimization collapse, where it heavily favors parameterless operators (like skip connections) over convolutions.
- **Search Space Bias**: Even with AutoML-Zero, the mathematical primitives and basic structure skeletons (`Setup`, `Learn`, `Predict`) represent significant human design biases. Evolving entirely new neural modules from scratch without these skeletons remains an open challenge.
