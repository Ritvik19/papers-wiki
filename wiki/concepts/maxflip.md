# MAXFLIP

**Type**: concept  
**Tags**: #concept

## Overview

**MAXFLIP** is an adversarial evaluation protocol for benchmarking answer stability in Large Language Models (LLMs). Introduced in *Who Flips? Self- and Cross-Model Counterarguments Reveal Answer Instability in LLMs*, MAXFLIP measures the maximum susceptibility of a model by selecting, for each question, the single most persuasive counter-argument generated across a diverse ensemble of peer models.

---

## Methodology

Standard counter-argument evaluations typically test models in a **self-challenge** regime (where model $M$ is challenged with a counter-argument generated by $M$ itself) or in a pairwise **cross-model** regime (where $M$ is challenged with arguments generated by a single specific peer $M'$).

MAXFLIP extends this by pooling coerced counter-arguments across an entire fleet of distinct model families and sizes $\mathcal{M} = \{M_1, M_2, \dots, M_n\}$:

1. For question $q$ and incorrect option $x$, each model $M_i \in \mathcal{M}$ generates a counter-argument $R_{M_i}(q, x, k)$.
2. For each target model $M$, MAXFLIP identifies the adversarial argument $R^*(q, x, k) \in \{R_{M_1}, \dots, R_{M_n}\}$ that maximizes the probability of eliciting an answer flip from $a^*$ to an incorrect option.
3. The model's answer flip rate under MAXFLIP is computed against this optimized set of per-question challenges.

---

## Empirical Findings

- **Substantial Flip Rate Surges**: MAXFLIP increases flip rates across every evaluated model compared to standard self-challenge ($k=10$), with mid-tier models experiencing surges of up to **$+23.6$ percentage points**.
- **Exposure of Latent Fragility**: Self-generated counterarguments only uncover flaws that the model itself is capable of conceiving; pooling arguments across diverse model architectures exposes latent reasoning blind spots and subtle rhetorical fallacies that single-model evaluations miss.
- **Model Diversity as an Adversarial Probe**: A heterogeneous pool of models (spanning different parameter scales, training data mixtures, and post-training alignment techniques) generates significantly more effective counterarguments than any individual model family operating in isolation.
