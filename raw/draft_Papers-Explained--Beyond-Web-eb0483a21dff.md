Papers Explained: Beyond Web

Recent advances in LLM pretraining show that simply scaling web data leads to diminishing returns, pushing researchers to use synthetic data. The BeyondWeb framework, through targeted rephrasing of high-quality web data and careful optimization of generation strategies, consistently yields superior model performance and faster training compared to the existing synthetic datasets.

Two Approaches for Synthetic Pretraining Data

Generator-Driven Approach

This method uses large-scale pretrained models to generate synthetic data from scratch, leveraging the knowledge contained in these models. For example, Tiny Stories showed that high-quality prompts with GPT-4 could successfully train small language models, challenging the trend of scaling models with web data. Phi models further developed this by using synthetic and raw web data to outperform much larger models, while Cosmopedia generated a large open-source synthetic dataset with diverse topics from open-source LLMs. However, relying on powerful generators is costly and inaccessible for many researchers. Problems include limited diversity, model collapse, and inherited biases or hallucinations from the generator.

Source Rephrasing Paradigm:

Proposed to overcome generator-driven limitations, this method rephrases existing web content using smaller models to produce higher-quality, more structured training data. WRAP (Web Rephrase Augmented Pre-training) reduced dependence on large models and sped up pretraining by over 3×. Other studies showed rewriting articles improved model performance and REWIRE converted medium-quality documents into high-quality for augmentation. This approach is widely adopted in industry and has become the main paradigm, with state-of-the-art LLMs reporting gains from rephrasing internet data. The shift is toward data-driven rather than model-driven synthetic generation.

Synthetic Data Beyond Pretraining

Synthetic data is also valuable in post-pretraining tasks such as fine-tuning and evaluation. It helps with task-specific objectives, aligning model behavior, reducing toxicity, and improving generalization. Techniques like instruction-tuning, Chain-of-Thought prompting, and data backtracing highlight its usefulness in reasoning, alignment, and reducing hallucinations. Despite this, the context focuses on the impact of synthetic data specifically for pretraining because it stands to make the most significant improvements.

BeyondWeb

BeyondWeb is a synthetic data method combining broad web-scale data coverage with targeted, strategically-generated content, specifically to fill gaps in underrepresented styles, formats, and topics. It uses:

Format transformation (e.g., into question–answer pairs)
Style modification (e.g., more pedagogical tone)
Content restructuring (to improve density and accessibility)

This design aims for models better aligned with real-world use and actual task requirements.

Baselines:

RedPajama: a well-established non-synthetic open web dataset, that has minimal curation applied to it.
Cosmopedia: 39M documents (textbooks, blogs, stories) generated by Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, latest version ~27B tokens, repeated if more data needed.
WRAP: Rephrases web content into alternative formats (esp. question–answering) using Llama-3.1–8b-Instruct.
Nemotron-Synth: High-quality synthetic subset of Nemotron-CC (~1.5T tokens; subset sampled for fairness). Generated by rephrasing high-quality, classifier-filtered content.

BeyondWeb’s own synthetic content is created from a high-quality subset of DCLM.

For comparison with synthetic datasets a mixed curation strategy is used: 60% RPJ + 40% synthetic data.

Training Details:

Trained LLM sizes: 1B (1T tokens), 3B (180B tokens), 8B (180B tokens).
Architectures: LLAMA-3.2 (1B, 3B); LLAMA-3.1 (8B).

Performance across all tasks averaged over 0-shot and 5-shot for different model scales and data curations.

Performance Results:

Consistent performance boost across model sizes: 1B: 57.4% accuracy (+6.7pp vs RPJ), 3B: 60.8% (+7.3pp), 8B: 63.7% (+7.1pp)
Outperforms Nemotron-Synth (strongest synthetic baseline): +3.1 to +2.6pp across sizes.
For 8B, matches/exceeds RPJ’s 180B-token performance in just 23.2B tokens (7.7× faster), and Nemotron-Synth’s in 66.2B tokens (2.7× faster); implies lower cost, energy, and faster iteration.
BeyondWeb establishes new state-of-the-art for speed–accuracy trade-off; e.g., their 3B model (60.8% accuracy) surpasses all but the strongest 8B baselines trained using the same token budget.
Gains are broad, not limited to specific benchmarks. Outperforms baselines on 13/14, 12/14, and 13/14 tasks at 1B, 3B, and 8B, respectively .

Systematically Evaluating Synthetic Data

Despite abundant web data for pretraining language models, relying solely on internet-sourced text creates several challenges:

Data Redundancy: Web data is finite and is being outpaced by model pretraining needs. Repeating data leads to diminishing returns and overfitting, so simply repeating data is not a solution for acquiring new information.
Style Mismatch: Most web data is in the style of personal blogs, product pages, and news articles. However, real-world model usage frequently involves conversational or instructional formats, creating a mismatch between training and deployment scenarios.
Knowledge Bottlenecks: Underrepresented domains on the web result in “blind spots” where the model lacks knowledge about certain topics.
Training Efficiency: Maximizing training efficiency depends on the quality and structure of tokens rather than just the number of tokens.

To address these, generating synthetic data becomes essential. However, not all synthetic data is equally effective. The research evaluates how synthetic data can:

Overcome data limitations
Better match training distributions to downstream tasks
Improve knowledge efficiency and diversity

Experimental setup:

Uses a subset of the RedPajama dataset (HQ Web) for high-quality web data.

Models (usually Llama-3.2–1B) are trained on 20 billion tokens, split 50:50 between real (HQ Web) and synthetic data.
Synthetic data is generally produced by rephrasing the same 10 billion tokens of HQ Web data, so the total “new” web knowledge remains fixed.
The baseline (RPJ-HQ) model sees the same 10 billion real tokens but sees them twice, not as rephrased synthetic data.
For other curated datasets like Cosmopedia, 10 billion random tokens are sampled for comparison.
Summary of key findings organized by theme.
RQ1: Are Generator-Driven Approaches Approximated by Summarization?

One hypothesis that may explain the benefits of synthetic data is that it effectively increases the per-token information density of training data. Cosmopedia uses LLMs to create pedagogically optimized, condensed materials based on their internal knowledge, similar to writing a textbook on a topic. Summarization, by contrast, uses an LLM to compress existing internet text while preserving essential information. We compared Cosmopedia (using an 8×7B model seeded with topics) to a simpler summarization method (using a single 8B model and a basic prompt).
Summarize the following text. Directly start with the summary. Do not say anything else.Knowledge transfer effectiveness across different synthetic data approaches.
Summarization (46.7% accuracy) almost matched Cosmopedia (47.1%), indicating that information condensation is a major factor in the success of synthetic data.
Even naive summaries improved performance over baseline by 1.2 percentage points, demonstrating that increased per-token information density benefits pre-training.
However, BeyondWeb, achieved even higher accuracy (50.4%), exceeding summarization and Cosmopedia by a notable margin. — This suggests that while summarization-based distillation captures many benefits, more sophisticated synthetic data generation methods can provide additional, significant improvements.

RQ2: Can Synthetic Data Break the Data Wall?

The main question is whether synthetic data can effectively overcome the limitations of scarce, high-quality real-world data.
Illustration of data splitting and corpus construction strategies to enable a controlled setup.
Three controlled datasets, each totaling 20 billion tokens, were used to compare training strategies:

Full Data (Upper Bound): Uses the complete available 20 billion tokens, without repetition or synthetic augmentation. This serves as the maximum achievable performance and is the ‘oracle baseline’.
2x Repeat (Lower Bound): Uses only 10 billion unique tokens, repeated to reach 20 billion tokens. Only the second half of each paragraph is kept, and this is repeated. This represents a naive, lower-bound solution using data repetition.
Continuation (Naive Synthetic): Also starts with 10 billion real tokens; the remaining 10 billion tokens are generated by an LLM, which is prompted to generate natural continuations of partial documents. The second half of original paragraphs is specifically chosen to avoid the LLM “cheating” by overfitting on seen samples.
Continue the following text in the same style as the original.Performance comparisons across different data augmentation strategies during training.
Repetition leads to performance degradation.
Naive synthetic generation provides only modest improvements.
Simple model-generated continuations appear sufficient to compensate for limited real data. An important caveat: the LLM used to generate continuations may have seen more data in its pretraining, allowing “new knowledge” to be added, which is a confounding factor.
BeyondWeb achieves 4.2 percentage points above the Full Data baseline. This shows that carefully crafted synthetic data can actually surpass the accuracy ceiling set by natural data alone.

RQ3: How Important is the Quality of the Seed Data for Rephrasing?

When generating synthetic data, is it better to use high-quality (even if repeated) inputs or to try to enhance low-quality inputs through rephrasing? Given a fixed amount (10B tokens) of high-quality data and abundant low-quality data.

High-quality subset (HQ Web): A small sample of top-tier data from RedPajama.
Low-quality subset (LQ Web): A random sample of the RPJ dataset.
Both HQ Web and LQ Web are rephrased by an LLM to create HQ Synth and LQ Synth, respectively.

Different training combinations are evaluated:

HQ Synth + HQ Web
LQ Synth + HQ Web
Baseline
Performance comparison across different quality combinations in training data.
The combination of HQ Synth + HQ Web reaches 49.2% accuracy, showing high-quality source material is critical when generating synthetic data.
LQ Synth + HQ Web scores 48.6%, which is lower than HQ Synth + HQ Web (49.2%), proving that the quality of input for synthetic generation matters more than making the data “novel” (i.e., less repetitive).
Still, using LQ Synth + HQ Web improves the score by +3.0 percentage points over the baseline.

RQ4: Is Distributional Style Matching Important?

Most web data comes from sources like blogs, news, and product pages, while real-world language model use cases (e.g., chatbots, customer support) rely on conversational, back-and-forth interactions. Analysis of the RedPajama dataset, using both GPT-4 and manual inspection of styles from “Organize the Web,” confirms that only 3.67% of web data is conversational in nature.

Synthetic rephrasing is proposed as a way to generate more examples in exactly the forms needed for downstream applications, addressing the mismatch. Three datasets are constructed using RedPajama with varying proportions of conversational data: 10%, 20%, and 50%, with the rest being randomly sampled data. Each dataset contains 20 billion tokens.
Effect of conversational data ratio on final accuracy.
As conversational content increases, accuracy gains are present but modest, and improvements show diminishing returns as the percentage rises
Style-matching can help improve the quality of synthetic training data, but is not a sufficient solution for maximizing performance by itself.

RQ5: How Important is Diversity at Scale?

Does increased diversity in synthetic data provide lasting benefits during extended model training, or do all generation strategies eventually plateau. Several synthetic data generation strategies are compared:

Cosmopedia: Generates textbook-like content related to web samples in a uniform, generator-driven style.
QA Wrap: Rephrases web documents into conversational Q&A.
Nemotron-Synth: Diversifies WRAP prompts, including various formats such as MCQ, Yes/No, open-ended, logical problems, etc.
BeyondWeb: Maximizes diversity in synthetic data, prioritizing learning-relevant styles and formats.

Training dynamics across model scales showing performance improvements relative to RedPajama baseline.

Approaches that use a mix of data styles/formats continue to yield new learning signals throughout training.
Single-strategy methods (like Cosmopedia) saturate quickly; their gains are limited after initial adaptation.
Diverse synthetic data is robust against overfitting even in extreme training regimes and enables language models to sustain learning and generalization.

RQ6: How Important is the Synthetic Rephraser Model Family?

Four generator models are used for rephrasing:

OLMo-2–7B
Phi-4–14B
Mistral-7B-v0.3
Llama-3.1–8B 

The same rephrasing prompt is used for each generator. 10B tokens of synthetic data are produced per generator and combined with 10B tokens of web data (20B total) for model training.
Synthetic data benefits are largely consistent across generator model families, and generator model quality does not predict synthetic data quality.
Synthetic data from all generator model families produces consistently high-quality training data. All results were within ±1pp of each other.
The primary driver of performance is not the generator’s general language modeling capability, but its ability to do rephrasing/data transformation, a skill apparently common across model families.

RQ7: Does Rephraser Size Matter?

Used Llama-3 models of three sizes: 1B, 3B, and 8B. Each generated 10B tokens of synthetic data via rephrasing, which was then paired with 10B tokens of web data, creating 20B token datasets for training.
Effect of generator model size on synthetic data quality.
Larger generators consistently yield better synthetic data and improve downstream performance.
Performance plateaus as generator size increases.
Rephrasing’s simplicity means model size is less critical, enabling highly scalable synthetic pretraining with smaller, cheaper models.
