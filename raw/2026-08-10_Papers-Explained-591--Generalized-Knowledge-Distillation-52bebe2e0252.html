Papers Explained 591: Generalized Knowledge Distillation

Knowledge distillation compresses a teacher model by training a smaller student model, but traditional KD struggles with distribution mismatch between training and inference outputs for auto-regressive sequence models. Generalized Knowledge Distillation addresses this by training the student on its self-generated sequences, using teacher feedback, allows flexible loss functions, and integrates distillation with RL fine-tuning.

The divergence between two probability distributions is a measure of the similarity of the distributions, with KL divergence a prevalent measure. The KL divergence between an approximating probability distribution Q(c) and a true probability distribution P(c) is given by: DKL(P ∥ Q) = Σc∈C P(c) log [P(c) / Q(c)].

The KL divergence is not symmetric: DKL(P ∥ Q) ≠ DKL(Q ∥ P). As such, DKL(P ∥ Q) is referred to as the forward KL, while DKL(Q ∥ P) is referred to as the reverse KL between P and Q.

Given model capacity mismatch, when approximating P(C) using a distribution Qθ©, minimizing the reverse and forward KL results in mean and mode-seeking behavior.

While KL divergence can be unbounded, a well-known divergence that is bounded even for probability distributions with disjoint supports is the generalized JSD (Jensen-Shannon divergence). JSD(β) interpolates between the forward and reverse KL using the bounded coefficient 0 < β < 1.

Given two auto-regressive sequence models of different capacity, pS and pT refer to the student and teacher respectively. The student has learnable parameters θ and pθS is differentiable with respect to θ. A dataset of inputs X is provided. Optionally, access to a dataset of input-output sequence pairs (X, Y) is assumed. If such a dataset is not given, it can be generated by sampling sequences from the teacher.

SFT: If only a fixed dataset of ground-truth output sequences is given, without query access to the teacher policy, a simple approach is to minimize the negative log-likelihood of such sequences under the student policy:

L_SFT(θ) = E_(x,y)∼(X,Y)[−log p_θ^S(y|x)].

Sequence-Level KD maximizes the likelihood of high probability sequences generated by the teacher, and can be viewed as supervised FT on teacher-generated outputs.

Supervised KD is a widely used technique where the student is trained to imitate the token-level probability distributions of the teacher. The student pS is trained with the supervised objective LSD over the target token-level probabilities of the teacher pT.

Generalized Knowledge Distillation

Commonly-used KD approaches use a fixed dataset of output sequences, either using ground-truth targets or teacher-generated sequences. However, distilling auto-regressive student models using such approaches results in train-inference distribution mismatch. To address this mismatch, imitation learning (IL) is leveraged, in particular on-policy imitation approaches, introducing on-policy KD. When using on-policy data during distillation, the student receives token-specific feedback from the teacher’s logits on the erroneous tokens in its self-generated output sequences. This enables a form of feedback loop akin to what is observed in RL, which helps minimize the train-inference distribution mismatch.

Given an input x, the student generates the output sequence y and imitates the teacher token-level distributions, pT(yn|x), on intermediate states y<n.

During training, a temperature of γ = 1 is used to encourage diversity in student generated sequences. Moreover, given unlabeled input prompts, generating sequences using the student is computationally cheaper than the teacher, due to differences in their model sizes.

Building further upon on-policy KD, a more general approach is proposed, termed Generalized KD (GKD), which unifies supervised and on-policy approaches. For output sequences, GKD uses a mixture of fixed dataset, either teacher-generated or ground-truth, and on-policy student-generated sequence:

While forward KL is commonly-used for distillation, it requires the student to cover the entire support of the teacher token-level distribution pT(.|y<n, x). In doing so, the student might end up assigning probability mass to tokens v which have low probability under pT(.|y<n, x), which can result in hallucination and low-quality generations. When the student has much lower model capacity than the teacher, this issue is likely to happen with temperature sampling. Alternatively, mode-seeking divergences, such as reverse KL, prioritize the tokens where the teacher assigns high probability, which can avoid low-quality generations but at the expense of less diverse generations for a given input. Experiments indicate that optimal divergence seems to be task-dependent.

Experiments

Student / Teacher Models: Supervised fine-tuned T5-XL (∼ 3B) serves as the teacher. For students, T5-small (77M), T5-base (250M), and T5-large (800M) are used.

GKD Variants: For choice of divergence D in GKD, forward KL, reverse KL, and three variants of JSD(β) are used: JSD (0.1), JSD (0.5) and JSD (0.9). For student data fraction λ, λ = 1 (On-policy), λ = 0.5 (Mixed) and λ = 0 (Supervised) are tried.

  • GKD consistently outperforms SeqKD and Supervised KD, with scalability to smaller student models.

Abstractive Summarization

The XSum dataset, which consists of news articles paired with human-written summaries, is used. Performance is evaluated using ROUGE-2 score of predicted summaries on the validation split of XSum. T5 models supervised fine-tuned on XSum serve as students for distillation, while the fine-tuned T5-XL acts as the teacher.

  • On-policy GKD with JSD (0.9) divergence outperforms other approaches (ImitKD, f-distill) in both greedy and temperature sampling scenarios.
  • GKD is data-efficient; on-policy GKD with only 5% of the training data (without ground-truth summaries) can outperform supervised KD and ImitKD trained on the full dataset.
  • The optimal divergence depends on sampling temperature: at higher temperatures, JSD(0.5) and JSD(0.9) offer higher quality but lower diversity; at lower temperatures, divergence choice matters less and diversity decreases.
  • Combining on-policy GKD with RL using textual entailment feedback substantially boosts factual consistency and summarization quality for student models, surpassing their teacher models.

Machine Translation

Performance on the validation split is reported using the BLEU score, which measures the similarity of machine-translated text to high quality reference translations.

  • Generalized JSD divergences perform better than forward or reverse KL but their performance gap reduces when using a larger student.
  • Using purely on-policy and mixed data distributions consistently outperform GKD variants only using a fixed supervised dataset, showing the importance of generating on-policy output sequences from the student.

Arithmetic Reasoning

GKD is evaluated in conjunction with chain-of-thought (CoT) on GSM8K.

  • When using only the fixed CoT dataset or mixing it with student-generated CoTs, performance consistently falls short of using solely the student-generated CoTs.
  • Forward KL performs quite well, similar to our findings on XSum with greedy sampling.
  • Reverse KL also performs well especially when training using only a fixed dataset.
  • Performance consistently improves as the proportion of on-policy data increases, provided that at least 25% of the data is on-policy.
  • On-policy GKD have superior performance compared to baseline KD methods, across all student size.

Task Agnostic Distillation

The aim is to enhance the distilled model’s proficiency to handle diverse tasks presented in the form of instructions. The FLAN T5-XL model is employed as the teacher and its knowledge is distilled into the FLAN T5-Base. The distillation process utilizes the comprehensive FLAN2021 instruction tuning dataset, which boasts 5.36 million examples spanning 62 distinct language understanding and generation tasks. The distilled T5-base student is evaluated on two held-out benchmark suites: MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) and BBH (BIG-Bench Hard).

  • On-policy GKD with reverse KL substantially outperforms supervised KD and ImitKD.
  • In the context of instruction tuning, we find that using reverse KL performs much better than forward KL.
  • The efficacy of reverse KL in instruction tuning may stem from its mode-seeking nature as it ensures that the model zeroes in on the main intent or behavior specified by the instruction.
