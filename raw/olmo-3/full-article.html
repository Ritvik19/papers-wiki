Papers Explained: Olmo 3

Olmo 3 is a family of state-of-the-art, fully-open language and thinking models at the 7B and 32B parameter scales with a diverse set of capabilities, including long-context reasoning, function calling, coding, instruction following, general chat, and knowledge recall.

  • Olmo 3 Base is trained as a foundation on which to build models with thinking and tool-use capabilities.
  • From Olmo 3 Base, Olmo 3 Think, is developed and trained to perform step-by-step reasoning by generating intermediate thinking traces before producing a final answer.
  • In addition, Olmo 3 Instruct 7B and 32B models are trained and tuned to produce shorter, more direct responses.
  • Olmo 3 RL-Zero 7B is a variant of Olmo 3 trained using RL directly from Olmo 3 Base. Olmo 3 RL-Zero enables researchers to study how base model data affects RL performance.
  • Olmo 3.1 Think 32B is the flagship reasoning model.

The models and data are available at HuggingFace.

Olmo 3 Base

Olmo 3 Base is developed in three stages of pretraining for up to 5.9T tokens, midtraining for 100 billion tokens, and the newly added long-context extension for 50B (Olmo 3 Base 7B) or 100B (Olmo 3 Base 32B) tokens.

Olmo 3 modeling and training largely follows that of OLMo 2. Training is conducted with a context window of 8192 tokens (increased from 4096 tokens for OLMo 2) during pretraining and midtraining stages. To support scalable pretraining at longer sequence lengths, and to keep inference costs manageable, a sliding window attention (SWA) pattern is introduced in which each token can attend to previous tokens in a window of size 4096. SWA is added at three out of every four layers, and it is ensured that the last layer always uses full attention.

Experimental Design and Evaluation

To support decision-making during base model development, a collection of benchmark suites called OlmoBaseEval is developed. Scores are aggregated over task clusters that group benchmarks by assessed capability. Proxy metrics for evaluating small-scale models are developed by identifying when capabilities “emerge” during training. The overall signal-to-noise ratio is improved by evaluating more examples from noisy tasks or even removing them entirely.

The clustering procedure requires a process to determine the similarity of two evaluations. This is done by collecting a pool of 23K benchmark scores from 70 external, open-weight models. Two benchmarks are assumed to evaluate similar constructs if they rank models similarly. Hierarchical clustering using Ward’s variance-minimization is performed, which iteratively merges evaluation scores to minimize the variance of scores between benchmarks within a cluster. The resulting task clusters are: MCSTEM, MCNon-STEM, GenQA, Math, Code, and Code FIM.

Stage 1: Pretraining

First, Olmo 3 Base is trained on Dolma 3 Mix, a 6T token pretraining data mix, with 3 key novelties:

  • New tooling for fast and scalable global deduplication at the trillion-token scale
  • Two new methods for optimizing selection of training tokens: token-constrained mixing and quality-aware upsampling;
  • A novel source of academic PDFs: olmOCR science PDFs, converted to linearized plain text using olmOCR

Web Data Pool
The primary source is CommonCrawl, with 104 dumps up to December 31, 2024. HTML artifacts are removed, and semantic text is extracted using Resiliparse. Data from DCLM-pool is leveraged where available.
A multi-step process removes spam, adult content, excessively short/long documents, documents with excessive symbols or insufficient alphabetic characters, documents with internal repetition, and documents containing common spam phrases. Language filtering keeps only English documents. Finally, sentence-level heuristics from Madlad400 are applied. This reduces the dataset size by 84.6%.

  • Exact Deduplication: Global deduplication based on document text hashes removes all exact copies (67% reduction).
  • Fuzzy Deduplication: MinHash-based deduplication identifies and removes near-identical documents (23% reduction).
  • Substring Deduplication: A novel fuzzy suffix-array-based method removes repeated substrings within documents (14% reduction).

Duplodocus, a native-rust toolkit, is developed for large-scale distributed execution of deduplication.

  • Topic Classification: WebOrganizer tool partitions the deduplicated corpus into 24 topics (e.g., “Adult Content”, “Politics”, “Science and Technology”).
  • Quality Classification: A fastText-based quality classifier assigns each document a quality score using various datasets as training examples.
  • Partitioning: Documents are first partitioned by topic, then within each topic partition, they are subdivided into 20 quality tiers (vigintile buckets). This results in 480 disjoint subsets.

The processed data pool consists of 8T tokens, partitioned into buckets based on topic and text quality. Further processing, including quality-based filtering and topic reweighting, is applied to generate a balanced, high-quality mixture for pretraining.

olmOCR science PDFs Data Pool
A novel dataset of academic PDFs is created, replacing the previous peS2o dataset. PDFs are “politely” crawled from academic sites and repositories, adhering to robots.txt and avoiding paywalls. The crawl, completed by December 2024, generates 238 million unique PDF documents.

  • PDFs are pre-filtered and text is extracted using olmOCR.
  • If olmOCR fails, Poppler’s pdftotext is used as a fallback. Documents requiring fallback for more than 1 in 250 pages are excluded.
  • Lingua language detector is used to retain only English documents and remove documents with excessive spam or SEO keywords.

This process yields a dataset of 160 million PDF documents.
Fuzzy duplicates are identified and removed using a MinHash algorithm, targeting document pairs with at least 75% similarity. This results in a corpus of 156 million documents, a removal rate of 2.3%.

Documents containing Personally Identifiable Information (PII) are removed. A document type-aware approach is used, considering the intended public dissemination of different document types.

  • Gemma 3 12B classifies documents for sensitive standalone PII or linked information.
  • Gemma 3 4B classifies document types based on the first 5,000 characters.
  • Rules are developed based on classification results to identify suitable and unsuitable documents for public dissemination.

This process removes 4.9% of the remaining pool, yielding a corpus of 148 million documents.

A round of heuristic filtering removes low-quality documents based on criteria such as:

  • Non-English documents not caught by the initial filter.
  • Documents with more than 30% tables.
  • Documents with more than 20% numbers.

Markdown tables are converted to HTML and URL references are removed. This results in a corpus of 108 million documents.
The corpus is partitioned into 24 topical buckets using the WebOrganizer topic classifier. The partitioned data is then passed to the mixing stage for further processing.

Code, Math, and other sources

  • Stack-Edu: An improved version of the-stack-v2 dataset, specifically curated for educational programming content from GitHub repositories. Data is partitioned by programming language.
  • arXiv (Proof-Pile-2): Documents from the RedPajama dataset with a cutoff date of April 2023, chosen for preserving original LaTeX notation.
  • FineMath: A subset of Common Crawl documents containing mathematical educational content, reprocessed to maintain proper mathematical notation. Documents with a quality score of at least 3 out of 4 are included, with a cutoff date of September 2024. This replaces the previous use of OpenWebMath.
  • Wikipedia and Wikibooks (Dolma): Both the “English” and “Simple” editions, with a cutoff date of March 2023. Processed using WikiExtractor to remove markup formatting, and documents with 25 or fewer words are excluded.

Sampling and Mixing over Data Pools
A swarm-based method inspired by RegMix, Data Mixing Laws, and CLIMB is used to train many smaller proxy models with different mixing ratios. These proxy models are evaluated on various tasks, and the results inform the optimal mix.
A novel procedure called conditional mixing is introduced to adapt the mixing strategy to evolving data landscapes. This involves treating the existing optimized mix as a virtual domain and re-running the base procedure over this virtual domain plus any new or modified domains. The mixing process prioritizes STEM domains within web text. Quality-aware upsampling is also employed, where higher-quality data is overrepresented in the mix.
The mixing process involves three rounds of conditional mixing.

  • Round 1: Focuses on optimizing the mix across 24 WebOrganizer categories within web text.
  • Round 2: Fixes the web text ratio at 75% and optimizes the mix of programming languages within the remaining 25%.
  • Round 3: Integrates the 24 WebOrganizer categories of the PDF data, conditioned on the previous mixes.

The optimized mixes are evaluated on various tasks, demonstrating improvements in performance compared to the natural data distribution. To monitor the performance of the 32B model during pretraining, the weights from four checkpoints are averaged. This technique helps to mitigate the influence of learning rate fluctuations on performance evaluation.

Stage 2: Midtraining

During this midtrain stage, we use 100B high-quality tokens sampled from a brand new data pool we introduce in this work, Dolma 3 Dolmino Mix. This midtraining data significantly expands and improves upon OLMo 2 Dolmino Mix

  • A new two-part methodological framework combining lightweight, distributed feedback loops on individual data sources, with centralized integration tests to assess candidate mixes on base model quality and post-trainability.
  • Expansion to targeted data curation efforts across code, math, and general knowledge QA domains.
  • More intentional inclusion of data types — instruction data and thinking traces. 

The primary tool is the microanneal methodology (introduced with OLMo 2), which involves:

  • Selecting a target dataset.
  • Sampling 5 billion (5B) tokens from it.
  • Matching with 5B tokens from web data.
  • Annealing (training) on the combined 10B-token mix. Performance is then compared to a baseline microanneal using only 10B web tokens, enabling efficient evaluation of dataset impact on model performance.

Integration tests are performed in parallel, involving full annealing runs on candidate mixes of 100B tokens for midtraining. These tests evaluate combined data source performance and effects of extended midtraining. Checkpoints from integration runs are instruction-tuned and evaluated on post-train suites to confirm that observed midtraining gains improve base model capabilities.

To maintain continuity with pretraining, we keep web and PDF data from the first stage of Olmo 3, albeit after filtering for higher quality documents; this approach prevents excessive shift in training data distribution.

Math Capabilities

  • Dolmino-1 math: 10.7B tokens from OLMo 2 Dolmino Mix Math, filtered for decontamination. Using 5B tokens, yielded +10.4 points in MATH, +38.2 in GSM8K.
  • TinyMATH: For 7500 MATH training examples, 100 new variants are generated per example; solutions generated in Python (TinyMATH-PoT), conversational English (TinyMATH-MIND). 1.14B tokens. Microanneal (+13.2 MATH, +13.9 GSM8K).
  • CraneMath: Replication of SwallowMath (rewritten FineMath4+ with Qwen3 due to licensing), 5.6B tokens. Microanneals +18.5 MATH, +27.4 GSM8K.
  • MegaMatt: Regenerated MegaMath-Web-Pro-Max (post-June 2023 data, rewritten via Qwen3), 3.88B tokens. Microanneals +8.0 MATH, +13.0 GSM8K.

Code Capabilities

  • Stack-Edu (FIM): 50% documents transformed via fill-in-the-middle (FIM) approach as in StarCoder2, sampled for educational value (top 20% buckets per language). Quality filtering and sampling improve performance vs. natural distribution.
  • CraneCode: Recreation of SwallowCode. Python data filtered for syntax/linter errors, rewritten for style and optimization. 18.8B tokens. Microanneals: +5.0 HumanEval (5B tokens), +13.5 (12.5B tokens).

QA and Knowledge Access

  • Reddit-to-Flashcards: Submission-comment pairs from academic subreddits rewritten (GPT 4o-mini) into MCQA pairs, seven formats. 5B tokens in microanneal: +2 points MCNon-STEM, +3 MMLU.
  • Wiki-to-RCQA: Wikipedia passages, QA pairs generated via Qwen2.5 32B Instruct, constraints inspired by RCQA datasets (reading comprehensions). 4.2B tokens in microanneal: +2 GenQA, improvements in DROP, SQuAD, CoQA.
  • Nemotron: Diverse QA pairs synth subset improves GenQA (+1.5), MCNon-STEM (+1.9), equal MCSTEM. Other Nemotron subdatasets not included.

Cross-Capability Instruction Data

  • Tulu3 SFT: Expanded instruction set, message concatenation via double newlines (not post-train tokens). Sampled from expanded set filtered from final Tulu3.
  • Flan: Subset included after microanneals showed QA task improvements, same as OLMo 2 preprocessing.

Cross-capability Thinking Traces

  • Meta-reasoning: Synthetic data for seven cognitive capabilities (self-awareness, evaluation, goal management, hierarchical organization, backward chaining, backtracking, conceptual reasoning). Data from augmented math/code problems with detailed annotations, thinking traces generated via GPT-4.1/o4-mini. +14 Minerva Math, +14 HumanEval, +20 MBPP.
  • Program-verifiable data: Programmatically generated tasks, thinking traces distilled and correctness filtered via output verifier. 250M tokens in 5B microanneal: +1–2 points GSM8K, MBPP.
  • OMR rewrite full-thoughts: GPT-4.1 rewrites OpenMathReasoning for clarity/flow/formatting, preserving reasoning. 850M tokens + equal web text: +5.5 MATH, +8.4 GSM8K.
  • Existing thinking traces: Filtered synthetic datasets across domains (math, code, sciences, humanities, puzzles). Improvements up to +8 GSM8K, ~+2 HumanEval, MBPP.

High-Quality Web and PDF Data

  • Stage 1 web data: Top 10% quality buckets, sampled by natural distribution, as optimal ratio yielded no improvement (p. 25).
  • Stage 1 olmOCR science PDFs: Further filtered version from earlier PDF documents.
  • Stem-heavy crawl: Web crawl (Sep 2024–Jun 2025) of selected scientific/educational domains. Final set filtered using quality score threshold (0.6), top 2.83% data, top 0.79% of Dolma 3. +2 points MCNon-STEM, MCSTEM, Math in OlmoBaseEval.

Prior research suggests that memorization happens most strongly near the end of training, so decontaminating at the midtraining stage (and for long-context extensions) is most effective. The decon package, developed for this purpose, operates in two phases:

  1. For every midtraining document, decon samples n-grams at regular intervals and checks if each n-gram matches any benchmark n-gram in the evaluation suite.
  2. If a match is found, it expands the matched text to count adjacent contaminated n-grams; if their number exceeds a threshold, the entire document is removed as contaminated.

Stage 3: Long-context Extension

Olmo 3 uses long documents from the olmOCR science PDFs pool, with additional filtering and synthetic data augmentation. This dataset is termed the Dolma 3 Longmino Pool.

The training mix consists of 34% long-context data and 66% high-quality short-context data (from Dolma 3 Dolmino Mix). Olmo 3 7B trains on this mix for an extra 50B tokens, while Olmo 3 32B trains for 100B tokens.

During extension, Olmo 3 applies the YaRN method to all attention layers and does not modify positional embeddings on sliding-window attention layers. It also uses document packing and inter-document masking.

Sourcing Long Context Data

The core long-context dataset comes from scientific PDFs scraped from the web, processed by olmOCR. Data is filtered by gzip compressibility: the top and bottom 20% by compressibility are removed.

Gzip compression exploits redundancy in text. Documents with a lot of repeated content (high redundancy) will compress more; those with less structure or randomness compress less. The dataset removes the top 20% most compressible documents (too repetitive, likely low-information) and the bottom 20% least compressible (potentially noisy, non-linguistic, or corrupted).

Synthetic Augmentation

  1. Documents (n tokens) are split into m partitions (8K–32K each), aligned with natural section breaks where possible.
  2. Each partition is tokenized; salient one/two-word noun phrases are extracted with tf-idf.
  3. For each noun phrase, the top 8 snippets are selected by tf-idf score. 4/ Noun phrases, snippets, and prompts are fed to a language model to create aggregation tasks.

For 32,768 ≤ n < 65,536 documents, this yields 2–8 partitions per doc. OLMo 2 Instruct 32B is used for generating aggregation tasks.

Two synthetic aggregation types:

  1. CWE (Common Word Extraction): Model is asked, via QA pairs, how many times common unigrams occur in the partition.
  2. REX (Rewriting Expressions): For each phrase/snippet, the model generates an aggregation task from 12 scenarios (e.g., summary, dialogue, flashcards, quiz, debate, game, movie scene, ELI5-explainer.

Training Recipe

  • Document packing: Instead of naive concatenation and splitting, “best-fit” document packing is used to minimize split documents, improve sequence length, and boost long-context benchmark performance.
  • Intra-document masking: Ensures each training sequence only attends to tokens from the same original document, preventing harmful cross-document attention patterns.
  • Model souping: The last three checkpoints (steps 10,000, 11,000, 11,921) are merged for the final Olmo 3 Base 32B long-context model.

Olmo 3 Think

High-quality reasoning data (Dolci Think) is curated. A three-stage training recipe (SFT, DPO, and RLVR) is harnessed. The OlmoRL approach is introduced, merging new algorithmic and engineering advances with a strong community platform of research in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards.

Supervised Finetuning with Dolci Think SFT

Dolci Think SFT is curated by compiling a large collection of prompts across a diverse set of skills from other open efforts. These prompts are substantially filtered and synthetically generated reasoning traces for their completions.

  • Math: Prompts sourced from OpenThoughts3 (using all available math prompts and maintaining 16X repetition) and SYNTHETIC-2. Incomplete traces are completed using QwQ-32B with up to 32K tokens, discarding examples still incomplete after regeneration.
  • Code: Prompts gathered from AceCoder, The Algorithms (Python subset), Llama Nemotron Post-training, and OpenCodeReasoning. Up to 16 responses per prompt are generated with QwQ-32B and filtered for correctness using GPT-4.1-generated test cases. Downsampling and regeneration for incomplete code responses are done similarly to math. Combined datasets are downsampled to 16 repetitions.
  • Chat & Safety: Sourced from the Tülu 3 subset of WildChat and OpenAssistant, plus unused WildChat prompts and Tülu 3 safety prompts. Reasoning traces and completions are generated using DeepSeek R1.
  • Precise Instruction Following: Prompts sourced from the Tülu 3 mix, and further constraints are added from “Generalizing Verifiable Instruction Following”. Persona IF prompts are regenerated with personas from Nemotron Personas. Responses are generated with QwQ-32B and verified for constraint adherence.
  • Science & Other: Science prompts come from the OpenThoughts3 science subset. Other data includes TableGPT (for data transformation) and Aya (for chat, multilinguality). Regeneration and reasoning chain generation are done as with math and code, using DeepSeek R1.

Heuristic filtering: Removed examples with

  1. unclear or non-commercial licenses
  2. incomplete reasoning chains
  3. domain-specific inaccuracies
  4. mentions of other model developers or date cutoffs
  5. excessive repetition
  6. excessive Chinese characters or Chinese political values

Topic filtering: Topics are classified using the OpenAI taxonomy and irrelevant or excessive topics (e.g., image generation requests, basic greetings) are filtered/downsampled to improve behavior.

Data Mixing methodology parallels midtraining: a small “base” mix of 100K OpenThoughts3 examples is evaluated for performance and compute efficiency. Individual models are trained on the base mix plus up to 100K examples from each category (no upsampling), and impact on evaluation is recorded. Final mix includes portions of all tested datasets, as each is helpful on at least one benchmark.

The Tülu 3 Decontamination Procedure/toolkit is used across all post-training data, filtering out instances matching evaluation sets via 8-gram matching with a 0.5 overlap threshold.

A learning-rate sweep is performed to select the best candidate checkpoints based on an evaluation suite. All models are then trained for two epochs to avoid overfitting. Each candidate checkpoint is tested with a series of qualitative “vibe-test” questions to inform the final checkpoint selection. The final thinking SFT checkpoint is a linearly weighted merge of two checkpoints trained with different learning rates.

Preference Tuning with Delta Learning

Prior work in general post-training has positioned preference tuning primarily as a means to improve alignment with human values and preferences. Preference tuning is rethought as a stage of contrastive learning that drives capability gains beyond what SFT alone can provide.

Further supervised finetuning on thinking traces generated by Qwen3 32B outright hurts the performance of Olmo 3 Think SFT, indicating that we are approaching saturation on learning from imitation. To extract a useful training signal out of these now-ineffective completions, Delta Learning’s principle is applied by pairing these completions with even worse responses (Qwen 3 0.6B, thinking). Minimizing the quality of the rejected completions (thus increasing the quality delta) yields a useful contrastive signal for preference tuning.

The intuition behind delta-learning is that the quality of preference data depends primarily on the quality of the delta between chosen and rejected responses rather than the quality of either response individually.

Dolci Think DPO is constructed by compiling a large pool of prompts covering a wide range of datasets and skills.

Topic filtering and heuristic model-identity filtering, as described during the SFT stage, are applied to all chosen responses. All prompts are decontaminated against the evaluation suites to prevent data leakage.

Models are trained for one epoch, sweeping learning rate and dataset size to identify the best candidate checkpoints based on the evaluation suite. Beyond the evaluation suite, each checkpoint is further inspected via the same “vibe-tests” as in SFT training to qualitatively assess model behavior.

Reinforcement Learning with OlmoRL

The following improvements are made over vanilla GRPO:

  • Zero gradient signal filtering: Groups of instances whose rewards are all identical (i.e., a batch with zero standard deviation in their advantage) are removed to avoid training on samples that provide zero gradient, similar to DAPO.
  • Active sampling: A novel, more efficient version of dynamic sampling maintains a consistent batch size despite zero gradient filtering.
  • Token-level loss: A token-level loss normalizes the loss by the total number of tokens across the batch, rather than per-sample, to avoid a length bias.
  • No KL loss: Removing the KL loss allows less-restricted policy updates, and this removal does not lead to over-optimization or destabilized training.
  • Clip higher: The upper-bound clipping term in the loss is set to a slightly higher value than the lower bound to enable larger updates on tokens.
  • Truncated importance sampling: To adjust for differences between log probabilities from the inference and training engines, the loss is multiplied by the truncated importance sampling ratio.
  • No standard deviation normalization: When calculating advantage, the standard deviation of the group is not used for normalization. This removes a difficulty bias, where questions with low standard deviation in their rewards (e.g., too hard or too easy) have their advantages significantly increased by the normalization term.

For verification purposes, several methods are employed:

  • Math: A rule-based verifier normalizes answers and compares them with a reference answer generated by SymPy. The verifier returns 1 if the answers match and 0 otherwise.
  • Code: A test-case based verifier runs a set of test cases over the response. Two approaches are experimented with: (a) rewarding based on the percentage of passed test cases, and (b) returning 1 if all test cases pass and 0 otherwise.
  • Instruction-following: A series of functions assess adherence to constraints specified in the prompt. A reward of 1 is given if all constraints are satisfied, and 0 otherwise.
  • Chat — reference: For tasks with a ground-truth response, an LM judge compares the model’s response against a provided reference answer and assigns a score between 0 and 1 based on the response quality.
  • Chat — open-ended: An LM judge evaluates the response without a reference answer and assigns a score between 0 and 1 based on the response quality.

The Dolci-Think-RL dataset contains about 100,000 samples from four domains: mathematics, coding, instruction following, and general chat, aiming to support robust RL for varied reasoning tasks

Difficulty filtering: Eight rollouts from the initial model checkpoint are generated per prompt; samples solved easily (>62.5% pass rate) are removed. Sampling matches RL training (temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0). Used fully for 7B model, while the 32B model relies on active sampling with GRPO group gradient.

Olmo 3 Instruct

Supervised Finetuning

Dolci Instruct SFT builds on the OLMo 2 Instruct mixture and improves general chat, reasoning, and function-calling capabilities.

Two types of trajectories are collected for function calling data:

Trajectories with Real Interactions:

Agents interact with MCP servers to answer queries, there is a single user turn, but multiple agent-environment interactions. Domains:

  • Science QA: Queries about scholarly content (paper content and citation graphs). Uses GPT-4.1-mini and ASTA Scientific Corpus (ASC) MCP server
  • Web search QA: Adapted from DR Tulu, combining benchmark and real-world queries from multiple sources. Uses GPT-5 agent with Serper API for Google search and webpage fetching

Only queries requiring search and verifiable, long-form responses are included (filtered using GPT-5).

Trajectories with Simulated Interactions (SimFC Dataset):

  • LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, GPT-5) generate full trajectories (user queries, responses, assistant messages).
  • Draws APIs/tool sets from datasets like xLAM, ToolACE, MCP servers.

Tool-use data standardized with OpenAPI specification for tool definitions and pythonic code blocks for function calls. Tool specifications are provided in system prompts, tool calls encapsulated with XML tags, environment outputs in a special role. Tokenizer’s vocabulary extended with dedicated special tokens for tags.

Curating Dolci Instruct SFT

  • Includes all new function-calling data, instruction following, science, and chat prompts.
  • Removes reasoning traces and special tokens from examples.
  • Updates older completions (GPT-3.5, GPT-4) with GPT-4.1 completions.

The SFT stage of Olmo 3 Instruct is trained starting from Olmo 3 Think SFT model.

Preference Tuning

Heuristic contrastive pairs are constructed by generating chosen responses with a large model (Qwen3 32B) and rejected responses with a small model (Qwen3 0.6B). GPT-judged preference pairs are additionally generated to add a further source of preference signal. Responses from weaker models are always present in the response set judged for each prompt, and the worst response is selected as the rejected completion to maximize the resulting delta.

A multi-turn preference dataset is curated with prompts synthetically extended from the Tülu 3-DPO dataset. Preference pairs differed in only the last turn of the conversation to avoid ambiguity in quality ranking between turns of the same conversation. Synthetic conversations are generated with two methods: 

  • self-talk, extending the original prompt into a multi-turn conversation with LLM-generated follow-up requests
  • synthetic-context, generating related, independent questions or paraphrases of the initial prompt to use as previous user turns with associated completions.

Learning rate, dataset size, and different length-control interventions are swept by creating datasets with differing token cutoffs for length filtering. The best-performing checkpoint of each length budget is selected, and the final Olmo 3 Instruct-DPO checkpoint is selected based on qualitative vibe tests and performance-vs-length analysis.

Reinforcement Learning

For the RL training stage, the pool of prompts from Dolci Think RL is modified. This modification involves utilizing less challenging datasets in the math and code domains, and skipping the offline difficulty filtering.

Olmo 3 RL-Zero

RLVR is performed from the Olmo 3 Base model over five domains: math, code, precise instruction following (IF), general chat etc.

All datasets are decontaminated against pretraining and midtraining data to avoid leakage and confounding.

For math, DAPO Math is deduplicated and only English examples are kept. Larger datasets (Klear-Reasoner, Open-Reasoner-Zero, Omega) are semantically clustered, and one representative question per cluster is selected. Further offline filtering removes prompts that the final base model solves perfectly (8/8 samples). Results in 13.3K fully decontaminated math prompts.

Code, instruction-following, and general chat are subsampled from Dolci Think RL.

Evaluation prompts are “cleaned” to remove extra formatting (e.g., \boxed{}), making them resemble training prompts.

Evaluation

Olmo 3 Base

  • Olmo 3 Base 32B is the top performer among fully-open models at the 32B scale..
  • Achieves double-digit improvements over other fully-open 32B models on Math and Code tasks, coming close to the best open-weight baselines.
  • On MCQA benchmarks, its STEM and Non-STEM scores are similar to Marin 32B and OLMo 2 32B, slightly behind leading open-weight models.
  • On GenQA tasks, it clusters with other top fully-open models and is only narrowly behind open-weight leader Llama 3.1 70B.
  • Olmo 3 Base 7B achieves the best Math and Code performance among fully-open 7B models, with significant margins over Marin 8B, Apertus 8B, and OLMo 2 7B.
  • Among open-weight models, trails only top-tier Qwen and Nemotron Nano models in Math and Code.
  • Performs on par with leading fully-open models in both STEM and Non-STEM MCQA areas.
  • On GenQA tasks, outperforms all but Marin among fully-open models, and all but larger models Gemma 2 9B and Llama3.1 8B among open-weight models.

Olmo 3 Think

  • Olmo 3 Think 32B is the best fully-open model at its scale, outperforming Gemma 2 27B, Gemma 3 27B, and Qwen 2.5 32B-Instruct, while closing the gap to leading open-weight models like Qwen 3 and Qwen 3VL, even though it was trained with six times fewer tokens.
  • Olmo 3.1 Think 32B (with extended RL training) shows substantial performance improvements on math, reasoning, and instruction-following benchmarks
  • Most other benchmark results remain stable except a 5-point drop on AlpacaEval
  • Olmo 3 Think-7B outperforms several prominent open-weight thinking models and matches the performance of the larger Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2, despite its smaller size.
  • However, it lags behind the Qwen 3 series on knowledge tasks, possibly due to the latter’s distillation training from a larger parent model.

Olmo 3 Instruct

  • Olmo 3.1 Instruct 32B exceeds most open models at similar scale, including Qwen 2.5 32B, Qwen 3 32B (No Thinking), Gemma 3 27B, and even Apertus 70B.
  • On the AIME 2025 benchmark, Olmo 3.1 Instruct 32B attains a score of 57.9, which is 36.6 points higher than Qwen 3 32B (No Thinking), and substantially narrows the performance gap with Qwen 3 VL 32B-Instruct.
  • Olmo 3 Instruct 7B outperforms Qwen 2.5–7B Instruct, OLMo 2 Instruct 7B, and Apertus 8B Instruct on the evaluation suite.
