Papers Explained 598: Compress Distil

The paper investigates post-hoc compression of long chain-of-thought reasoning traces generated by large teacher models before knowledge distillation. Compressing these traces generally maintaining up to 96% of the accuracy of raw traces. However, raw traces consistently yield the highest downstream accuracy, and compressing traces offers an accuracy efficiency trade off rather than a free accuracy boost.

Method

Compress-Distill is a three-stage pipeline:

Trace Generation: Given a teacher MT and benchmark questions qi, a response ri = MT(qi) is sampled that contains a reasoning trace ti and a final answer ai. This sampling is repeated up to K times. Only correct traces are retained.
Trace Compression: For each correct triple (qi, ti, ai) from Stage 1, a compressor MC is applied with a single generic prompt that asks for a shorter version of the reasoning while preserving the essential logical steps, key insights, and the final answer. The compressed trace ˆti = MC (qi, ti, ai) is generated with low temperature (τ =0.3). The per-example character-level compression ratio ρi = |ˆti|/|ti| is measured.
Student Training: Student models MS are fine-tuned on raw traces, compressed traces, or an answer-only ablation that removes the entire … block, using a chat template:
User: qi → Asst: ⟨think⟩ ˜ti ⟨/think⟩ ai,
where ˜ti ∈ {ti, ˆti, ∅} (the last replaces the think block with the empty string, leaving only the final answer).

Methods

LoRA
Full fine-tuning
In-distribution benchmark datasets.
Trace generation and downstream evaluation use the in-distribution datasets. Downstream evaluation also includes OOD-reasoning datasets (HellaSwag, WinoGrande, PIQA, ARC-Easy, BoolQ, TruthfulQA) and OOD-knowledge groups (MMLU humanities, social sciences, other).

LoRA runs use rank 64, α=32, dropout 0.05, all attention and MLP projections, LR 1×10−4 with cosine schedule and 10% warmup, one epoch, and 8-bit AdamW. Full fine-tuning uses LR 2×10−5 for one epoch; the 8B, 9B, and 20B students use FSDP v2 with full sharding and activation checkpointing, while the 0.8B model fits on a single GPU.

Evaluation
Trace generation results.
On easy arithmetic (MultiArith, 99.0%) the Qwen3.5–397B-A17B teacher emits short traces (mean 1,750 characters), while on graduate-level science (GPQA Diamond, 68.2%) it averages 18,734-character traces, more than 10× longer.
The gpt-oss-120B teacher (high reasoning) answers with similar accuracy as Qwen3.5–397B-A17B, but substantially less verbosity (roughly half).
Compression statistics by (teacher, compressor, dataset).
Under the Qwen teacher, Llama-3.3–70B-Instruct compresses traces to a mean ratio of ρ=0.142, while Ministral-3–14B-Instruct-2512 is more aggressive at ρ=0.086.
Under the gpt-oss teacher, compression is milder, with mean ratios of ρ=0.210 for Llama-70B and ρ=0.147 for Ministral-14B.
Compression is strongly dataset-dependent. GPQA Diamond compresses hardest, whereas shorter/easier datasets such as MultiArith and ARC leave less room for compression. This pattern suggests that longer reasoning traces contain more removable deliberation.
Overall downstream accuracy for reasoningtrace runs.Per-student accuracy vs. median reasoning token count.
Raw traces consistently yield the highest downstream accuracy under all teachers and model sizes, followed by Llama-70B-compressed, then Ministral-14B-compressed.
Truncation (simply cutting raw traces to match compressed-token budgets) is less effective than rewriting/compression, except in some large model/budget settings. -Answer-only supervision (dropping all reasoning) is fastest/cheapest but leads to much worse and often unstable accuracy, especially for smaller models.
