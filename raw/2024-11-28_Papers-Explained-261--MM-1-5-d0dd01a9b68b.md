Papers Explained 261: MM-1.5

MM-1.5 is a family of multimodal large language models that builds upon the MM1 architecture. It adopts a data-centric approach to model training, systematically exploring the impact of diverse data mixtures across the entire model training lifecycle. This includes high-quality OCR data and synthetic captions for continual pre-training, as well as an optimized visual instruction-tuning data mixture for supervised fine- tuning. Additionally, two specialized variants are introduced: MM1.5-Video, designed for video understanding, and MM1.5-UI, tailored for mobile UI understanding.

Recipe for Building MM1.5
Recipe for building MM1.5.
A systematic analysis and categorization of datasets used in Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) are conducted based on the capabilities they aim to enhance. The ratio of each dataset category within the final mixture is carefully adjusted to achieve a balanced development of core capabilities.

To bolster performance, particularly in text-rich image understanding, a continual pre-training stage is introduced. This stage utilizes 45 million high-quality OCR data and 7 million synthetic captions generated by a pre-trained MM1-based image captioner. This approach aligns with similar strategies employed in VILA2 and LLaVA-OneVision.

For benchmarks like MMMU, which require extensive knowledge, the pre-training data is refined. Existing image-caption and interleaved image-text data from MM1 are retained while the text-only data is updated and the data mix ratio is meticulously adjusted.

The impact of dynamic image splitting, known as AnyRes, on high-resolution image comprehension is also investigated.

Data Mixture Ratio Study

Single-Image Data

The experiment begins by mixing general and text-rich data, which performs well across benchmarks. Upsampling/downsampling is then introduced to balance the number of samples from different categories (e.g., science, math, code) relative to the general category. The “MMBase score” (average of general, text-rich, and knowledge average scores) and “Refer&Ground average score” are used to determine the optimal ratio (α) for mixing different categories.
Impact of α for different data categories to a model’s different capabilities.
For science, math, and code, the best α values are 0.1, 0.5, and 0.2, respectively.
For refer&ground data, α = 2.0 is chosen as a good trade-off between MMBase and Refer&Ground scores.

Multi-Image Data

This research explores the impact of mixing single-image, multi-image, and text-only data by varying the sampling weights (wsingle, wmulti, wtext).
Impact of the mixing ratio for text-only and multi-image SFT data.
Varying wtext (text-only data) has minimal impact on the model’s base capabilities.
wtext is set to 0.1 to prioritize single-image data.
Increasing wmulti (multi-image data) leads to a decrease in base capabilities but an increase in multi-image benchmark performance.
wmulti is set to 0.1 to balance these factors.

Text-Only Data

Three mixture combinations are presented:

Base mixture: General, text-rich, science (αscience = 0.1), math (αmath = 0.5), and code (αcode = 0.2) data.
Single-image mixture: Base mixture + refer&ground data (αrg = 2.0).
All mixture: All data from single-image, multi-image, and text-only categories, with wsingle = 0.8, wmulti = 0.1, and wtext = 0.1.

The All mixture achieves the best overall performance by balancing all capabilities across benchmarks.

Continual Pre-training Ablations
Ablation study of continual pre-training.
Impact of image resolution

Two baselines are established: continual pre-training with 378×378 and 756×756 image resolutions. Image splitting and position embedding interpolation are adjusted based on the resolution setting. The final SFT stage always used a 1344×1344 image resolution. Performance is evaluated across different resolutions used in continual pre-training.

Continual pre-training with 1344×1344 image resolution yielded the best overall performance.
Decreasing the resolution consistently led to lower final scores.
Continual pre-training with 378×378 resolution could even underperform a model without continual pre-training.
This suggests that higher resolution images provide sufficient detail for the model to effectively learn from document-based OCR data during continual pre-training.

Impact of OCR data and synthetic captions

Two synthetic caption datasets, LLaVA-Recap-3M and ShareGPT4V-PT, are used in conjunction with OCR data for continual pre-training. Data is equally sampled from each dataset within each training batch. Performance is compared to a baseline model without continual pre-training.

All continual pre-trained models outperformed the baseline model.
No conclusive evidence is found that synthetic captions significantly improved performance over OCR data.

Dynamic Image Splitting Ablations

To effectively process images of variable aspect ratios and resolutions, a dynamic image splitting method for high-resolution image encoding is introduced. In addition to the sub-images, the original image with a longer side resized to the encoder resolution r is always fed to the model. This ensures that the model has a global understanding of the image. Two variants are considered:

before: the overview image is put before the sub-images.
after: the overview image is put after the sub-images.

These variants yield different results because an autoregressive mask is used in the LLM decoder.

The impact of indicating the position of each sub-image in the original high-resolution image to ensure the model can understand the original 2D image structure is explored. Two methods are considered:

Index. A tuple of (k, i, j) is used to represent sub-image position information, where k is the zero-indexed image number in the example, i and j are the one-indexed row and column id. i.e. e.g., (0, 0, 0) is the overview image of image 0.
Seps. Instead of using indexes, three text tokens are used. Specifically, ‘:’ is the overview image indicator, ‘,’ is the column separator, and ‘<n>’ is the row separator.

Final Model and Training Recipe

The same architecture as MM1 is used, focusing on the 3B dense model for all the ablation studies.
The overview of model architecture.
The pre-training data comprises three parts: (i) 2B image-text pairs, (ii) 600M interleaved image-text documents with 1B images in total, and (iii) text-only data with 2T tokens. The data ratio has been adjusted from 45:45:10 to 50:10:40, significantly downweighting the interleaved data (from 45% to 10%) while increasing the proportion of text-only data (from 10% to 40%). Except for the updated text-only data, the data remains unchanged from MM1.
45M OCR data is used to enhance text-rich image understanding during the CPT stage. Additional synthetic image captions are not included based on empirical results.
The final mixture for SFT consists of 80% single-image data, 10% multi-image data, and 10% text-only SFT data. The single-image data can be further categorized into 37.2% text-rich data, 22.5% refer&ground data (visual QA data enriched with bounding boxes and/or point coordinates), 11.3% general data, 5.6% math data, 2.3% code data, and 1.1% science data, totaling 80% of all used data.

The image grid configuration (n_min, n_max) = (4, 9) is used with an index for the sub-image position indicator and placing the overview image after the sub-images. Dynamic image splitting is only enabled when the current training sample has fewer than three images. The supported resolution reaches up to 4 Megapixels (approximately 2016×2016 for a square image, or 6048×672 for a long image).

The image encoder and the LLM backbone are kept unfrozen during all the model training stages.

As in MM1, the dense model is converted to MoE by replacing only the dense language decoder, keeping the image encoder and vision-language connector unchanged. Two MoE models are introduced, a 1B-MoE and a 3B-MoE, with 64 experts replacing dense layers every two layers.

MM1.5-Video

MM1.5-Video takes a video and an instruction as input and generates the response. For the inputs, N frames are uniformly sampled from the video at an arbitrary length and fed into the model as multi-image inputs without special frame assembly. Due to the token limits, dynamic image splitting for each frame is disabled, and the vision encoder generates the feature maps frame-by-frame independently. Specifically, 24 frames are sampled for each video, and each frame is represented by 144 tokens.

Two variants for MM1.5-Video are introduced.

First, MM1.5-Video is built as a training-free model, which is achieved by directly adopting the pre-trained MM1.5 image models to video tasks without being fine-tuned on any video data.
Second, the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) model is introduced where MM1.5 image models are fine-tuned on video instruction-tuning datasets to improve its temporal modeling capability for video tasks. A mixture of public video datasets from ShareGPTVideo (556K), VideoChat2 (225K), and ActivityNet-QA (31.5K) are used. These datasets contain a variety of video types, spanning different tasks (e.g., open-ended and multiple choice questions), viewpoints (e.g., first- and third- person views), and lengths (e.g., videos from a few seconds to tens of minutes).

MM1.5-UI

MM1.5-UI is an MM1.5 model variant further fine-tuned specifically on UI data that achieves competitive performance on UI understanding tasks and establishes new state-of-the-art performance in various benchmarks. MM1.5-UI models are trained by further fine tuning the final MM1.5 models on the Ferret-UI data mixture.
Comparison with SOTA models on UI benchmarks.
MM1.5-UI outperforms prior best models in nearly all benchmarks except Screen2words.
MM1.5-UI achieves a 9.1 point average performance improvement over Ferret-UI on iOS tasks.
MM1.5-UI demonstrates a clear hierarchy of difficulties among tasks, with text-based tasks being the most challenging, followed by icon-based tasks, and widget-based tasks being the easiest.
Higher resolution and dynamic image splitting in MM1.5-UI significantly benefit performance in icon-related tasks.
Fine-tuning MM1.5 on a mixture of general domain, text-rich, and refer&ground tasks (SFT) leads to superior UI performance compared to a baseline UI model fine-tuned with UI data only.
Performance generally improves with model scaling, but gains are modest, suggesting potential limitations due to data diversity, image resolution, or overfitting.
