Papers Explained: Arcee Trinity

Arcee Trinity is a family of sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models, including Trinity Nano (6B total/1B activated per token), Trinity Mini (26B/3B), and Trinity Large (400B/13B), featuring interleaved local and global attention, gated attention, depth-scaled sandwich norm, and sigmoid routing. Trinity Large introduces a novel load balancing strategy (SMEBU), is trained with the Muon optimizer for high efficiency and stability, and all models were pre-trained on massive datasets (Nano/Mini: 10T tokens, Large: 17T tokens).

Architecture

Tokenizer

A custom 200,000-token BPE vocabulary is trained on approximately 48GB (∼10B tokens) drawn primarily from the pretraining corpus for Trinity Nano and Mini. This dataset is supplemented with multilingual text from C4 non-English splits and a mix of instruction-following, reasoning-trace, and code data. The pretokenizer employs a multi-stage pipeline inspired primarily by the DeepSeek V3 tokenizer.

  1. Digit isolation and place-aligned chunking: Contiguous digit runs are first separated from surrounding text, then split into right-aligned groups of three with a leading group of one to two digits.
  2. Script-aware isolation: DeepSeek V3’s CJK isolation is extended to additionally cover Thai, Lao, Khmer, Myanmar, and Korean hangul/jamo. All of these scripts, like CJK, lack whitespace-delimited word boundaries. Isolating these script runs ensures BPE learns merges within each script independently rather than forming cross-script tokens.
  3. Word and punctuation splitting. The research adopts the DeepSeek V3 main text regex directly. This regex handles leading-space word attachment, punctuation-prefixed tokens (covering contractions, code sigils, and markup), and whitespace/newline normalization.
  4. Byte-level fallback. A byte-level encoding layer ensures full coverage of any byte sequence without unknown tokens.

Pre-Training

Data

All pretraining data for the Trinity models was curated by DatologyAI. Trinity Nano and Trinity Mini were trained on the same 10 trillion token mix distributed across 7T tokens in phase 1, 1.8T tokens in phase 2, and 1.2T tokens in phase 3, while Trinity Large was trained on 17 trillion tokens sampled proportionally from a distinct 20 trillion token mix (13T in phase 1, 4T in phase 2, 3T in phase 3).

Each phase consists of the same mixture, but the distribution of math and code data is boosted in later phases, in order to increase the final performance of the model.

The 10 trillion mix reused the AFM-4.5B dataset and added more math and code, targeting general English, programming, STEM, and reasoning skills.

The 20T Trinity Large mix featured advanced programming, STEM, reasoning, and multilingual curation targeted at Arabic, Mandarin, Japanese, Spanish, German, French, Italian, Portuguese, Indonesian, Russian, Vietnamese, Hindi, Korean, and Bengali.

A major aspect was the generation of over 8 trillion tokens of synthetic data, including 6.5 trillion tokens of synthetic web content produced by rephrasing high-quality seed documents using format transformation, style modification, and content restructuring. About 1 trillion tokens of synthetic multilingual data were generated similarly. Additionally, 800 billion tokens of high-quality synthetic code data were created by rephrasing selected code files for diversity and relevance.

Context Extension

Rapid iterative testing was done to evaluate context extension, using the Multi-Key Needle-in-a-haystack (MK-NIAH) task from RULER at the target context length as a metric. Consistent with previous studies, training at a sequence length longer than the target context window led to better performance at the target window. For Trinity Nano, training at 128K length yielded an MK-NIAH (@128K) score of 0.38, but training at 256K resulted in 0.548. Improved dataset selection and hyperparameters pushed this to 0.864.

Trinity Mini, trained at 128K for a 128K window, performed much better than Trinity Nano at 128K alone, reaching an MK-NIAH (@128K) score of 0.888. The trend of model size affecting ease of context extension persisted with Trinity Large. It was trained at 256K for a 256K window, achieving MK-NIAH (@256K) score of 0.994. When evaluated at 512K, it scored 0.976, even though not trained at that sequence length; at 1M, it scored 0.42, suggesting strong performance at yet larger windows is feasible.

There were no improvements from progressively increasing window size versus training directly at the longest sequence, starting from the final pre-trained checkpoint.

The long-context extension dataset contains ~117 billion tokens from 35.6 million documents. The sampling strategy is length-biased, with probabilities scaling by document length (from 1% up to 90% for 128K tokens), blending original pretraining data with targeted long-context sources. OCR-derived PDF data (olmOCR and FinePDF-edu), whole code repositories (regenerated ProLong datasets), instruction-style data (FLAN, math, code), AutoMathText, and curated arXiv/textbook sources were included. This mix maintains representativeness, instruction-following, few-shot learning, and enhances long-context generalization and code understanding.

Post Training

An instruction-tuning mix is built from a blend of public instruction data and custom data. The custom portion combines human-written prompts with synthetic instructions generated by stronger teacher models. These are then filtered for quality, formatting, and length. A large share of the coding subset comes from trajectories collected through agentic harnesses, in particular OpenCode.

For the supervised fine-tuning stage, the Cut Cross-Entropy kernel is used to reduce memory requirements. After supervised fine-tuning, a short RL stage is run using prime-rl.

Evaluation

  • Trinity Large Base achieves competitive scores with GLM 4.5 Base, despite having 4× higher degree of sparsity and a roughly 2.5× lower active parameter count.
